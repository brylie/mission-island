# Mission Island second-island brief

Build a wholly new island that players can travel to after progressing on the starter island. Aim for about **0.4 km² of playable dry land**, roughly ten times the starter island’s **0.038 km²**. Grow that footprint if the road network and runway need more room. Report land area in square kilometres and keep one world unit equal to one metre. Give the new island its own coastline, identity, and three villages. Seabrook, the harbor, and the original jobs stay on the starter island.

Make the second island explorable as a development preview. Give it an arrival landmark, such as a working ferry landing with room for a vehicle to disembark. Keep its terrain, site coordinates, and scenery independent so it can eventually be placed in a continuous world or loaded as its own map. Record what the future travel unlock will need and leave the progression threshold, travel mechanism, and world loading strategy open.

Start with a connected layout. Give the coastal village a working waterfront and fish market, a farming village a farmers market and nearby fields, and an upland village access to forest and industrial work. These are suggested roles. Connect the villages with continuous roads and give them distinct approaches using hills, rivers, lakes, forest, coast, and bridges. Include a secondary route or scenic way back. Shape roads and bridge approaches to suit loaded trucks, with smooth grades, usable curves, and room to turn around at work sites. Plan shoreline access for fishing and future boat rescues.

Make a map of sites and implement the first batch of recognizable exterior assets. Plan room for every site below, and record any models still queued after the first pass.

- Civic sites include a fire station, hospital, police station, church, school, and cemetery. Allow room for emergency vehicle pull-through and a helipad near the hospital or rescue base.
- Village sites include the fish market, farmers market, grocery store, and apartment building. Give each market an accessible delivery area.
- Industrial sites include a logging and lumber processing facility with a log yard, and a mine with an ore loading area. Place a small airport on suitably flat ground, with a runway, taxi or staging area, and clear approaches.
- Create or plan reusable exterior models for an ambulance, fire truck, police car, heavy log and ore trucks, a helicopter, and a small airplane. Include scale and orientation, wheel contact points for road vehicles, cargo dimensions where relevant, and pickup, parking, or landing locations. Stage models in the Asset Workbench when practical.
- Give the landscape a mix of conifers, deciduous trees, wetland reeds, meadow flowers, crops, and shore plants. Choose a few representative animals per biome, such as woodland birds and small mammals, wetland waterfowl, coastal seabirds, and fish. Use simple ambient motion and share or instance art where it makes sense.

Use the existing warm, original toy-town art direction. Keep the island easy to read from overview and judge believable steps, doors, cargo areas, and vehicle scale from third person. Give the new map its own extent, land and water bounds, biome names, and discoverable landmarks. Keep terrain generation deterministic and check meshes, terrain height, road decks, collision surfaces, and wading and boating edges against each other. Use the Landscape Workbench or the second-island preview to verify slopes, bridge joints, waterfalls, building foundations, and loading areas. Measure the approximate land area and compare preview frame rate and loading time against the starter island on the same machine. Prefer distance-based detail and reusable assets.

Work on a separate branch or worktree from the shared playtest baseline. Own the new island’s landscape, routes, scenery and location data, assets, placement, and preview. A parallel change owns the flatbed model, driving input, multiplayer truck state, truck and cargo physics, and brake lighting. Put new vehicle exteriors in separate modules and flag integration points with shared rendering, saves, and physics. Keep starter-island coordinates and saves compatible. The new sites should provide the space and assets for future jobs and vehicles.

Deliver a preview tour from the arrival point through all three villages, a site and asset inventory with map coordinates and remaining work, and a short QA report. Include dry land area in km², routes checked, any physics integration needed for bridges or loading areas, measured performance, starter save compatibility, and test results.
