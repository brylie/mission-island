# Creative direction and inspirations

The intended feeling is **being useful in a place you know**. On the first morning the striped awning helps you find the market. A few visits later you use it to give your crewmate directions. In the planned resident loop, arriving just before the shopkeeper walks home gives that same corner a time of day as well as a name.

## Themes players can experience

| Theme | A design choice | A question to ask in play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cooperation | A delivery has several useful roles. Someone can load, drive, unload, or earn money fishing while the other finishes the route. | Can each player explain what their partner contributed? |
| Belonging | Give people and equipment a usual place. Let a repaired tide staff, a cottage herb box, or a resident’s walk to work reveal who tends the island. | Can a returning player give directions using a detail they remember? |
| Stewardship | Let useful work leave evidence. A planned stage of the ferry repair should be visible from the depot road the next morning. | Could the crew point to what changed after their contribution? |
| Curiosity | A worn path reaches the edge of the sand. From there the staff and the shape of the rocks invite a closer look. A different tide or animal behavior can invite a second visit. | What would make either player pause here on the way to a job? |

## Style at two distances

**From the island view**, read the shape and purpose first. A truck needs a cab and a legible cargo bed. A harbor needs a road that leads the eye to its working edge. **At walking distance**, spend detail where hands, feet, and eyes will go. Show how a side rail attaches, leave space for two people beside a bench, and give the path a convincing ending. Use metres for dimensions and compare a prop with the crew and truck before adding more detail.

Materials should suggest how something grows, weathers, or is handled. Look at wood grain along a post, gravel at the edge of a path, and wear where boots reach a landing. Describe controls in those terms. Favor a few readable motions, such as a bobbing buoy or a hare breaking cover, and check that another player can still drive and read the road while they are visible. The [world survey](../living-world-qa.md) gives actual locations for these checks.

For sound, a direction to try is a cue with a recognizable source: boots change tone at the dock, or a saw becomes audible on the approach to a timber yard. Pair important prompts with visible feedback so two players talking across the room can still follow the action. Start by listening for surf, surface footsteps, and crate impacts on the route from Harbor Depot to Driftwood Beach. ### Give forms a reason to look the way they do

Treat the landscape like a painter working from observation. Read the masses of color, where light lands and what shape leads the eye before adding surface detail. Keep a soft, legible toy-scale silhouette from above. At walking distance, let the curve of a branch, the changing edge of a wetland or the wear at a cottage threshold reward a closer look.

For pines, shape a tapered trunk and branches with weight. A branch can leave the trunk on a shallow rise, dip under its foliage and lift at the growing tip. Follow a species reference for the actual curve and vary branch length, spacing and angle. Build the crown from irregular foliage masses with openings where branches divide. Even a bushy round silhouette can have arcing stems and lobes that overlap, with sheltered specimens fuller and exposed crowns bent by the wind. Carry that gesture and the main openings into distant silhouettes. Compare a young tree and a mature one, then look at several seeded trees together for repeated outlines.

Use the same reasoning for rocks, ground and built scenery. A boulder can have a fractured face and settle into the soil. Grasses can follow damp ground around its base. A workshop can keep a straight dock edge where a truck needs it, with a soft verge and worn foot route where people approach. Spend geometry on the readable shape and use materials for smaller marks. Keep procedural controls tied to visible traits such as branch droop, crown openness, exposure, age and season.

Start the art pass with a sheltered Pinewoods pine and an exposed ridge pine. Compare a reference photo in the Workbench, then review both in place, with clear and overcast light and at walking and overlook distance. When seasons are available, capture the same scene twice before adding any new detail. Both versions should still read as the same place. Use the [Art Director skill](../../.agents/skills/mission-island-art-director/SKILL.md) to record the review. [Island climates and seasons](climate-and-seasons.md)

### Grass seen from a hill

At a player's feet, individual blades can catch the light. From Windstone Ridge, the meadow should read as a few broad changes in colour that tell you something about the land. Try a greener strip following the damp ground beside a stream and a paler shoulder where the slope is exposed. Keep their edges irregular and soft enough for a path or a grazing animal to stand out. The meadow already fades fine detail with distance and varies its colour in world space. Use those controls to judge which repeated marks still catch the eye.

Start with the saved meadow views at walking, hillside and island distances. Match camera, hour, weather and quality between revisions, then repeat under a second light. Walk down the slope and orbit it. Look for blades close up, a continuous transition into larger patches, and patches that stay anchored as the camera moves. If a row of identical marks still stands out, capture the first distance and angle where you can trace it. ## What we take from our references

| Reference | Quality we want in Mission Island |
| --- | --- |
| **Good Job!** | Clear practical objectives with room to improvise. A paid delivery can survive a clumsy first attempt, and a partner can solve the same obstruction another way. [Nintendo](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/good-job-switch/) |
| **Farming Simulator** | Work that progresses through identifiable steps and equipment with a purpose. A harvest becomes a larger barrow order. A later farming tool changes how the crew handles it. [GIANTS manual](https://manuals.giants-software.com/Farming_Simulator_25/Basegame/lang/en/FS25-manual_EN.pdf) |
| **Totally Reliable Delivery Service** | Physical cargo and a co-op mishap you can recover from together. Make the difference between throwing, hand loading, and securing a load easy to see. [Official game site](https://www.totallyreliable.com/) |
| **Stormworks: Build and Rescue** | Rescue work where the vehicle, weather, and crew roles shape the response. Build from small local jobs toward equipment and responsibilities players know how to use. [Geometa’s game page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/573090/Stormworks_Build_and_Rescue/) |
| **Stardew Valley** | Familiar days, resident relationships, and skills that open new work. Let the crew build Trust through useful deeds and see the island change through a shared project. [Official game overview](https://www.stardewvalley.net/about/) |
| **Animal Crossing** | The comfort of ordinary routines and the freedom to make a place and a wardrobe feel like yours. Keep the appeal of visiting the same spot at another hour or in a different season. [Nintendo’s island guide](https://animalcrossing.nintendo.com/new-horizons/explore/) |
| **Micro Machines** | The delight of reading a small vehicle and its route from above. Preserve that silhouette when adding functional exterior details. |
| **Human Fall Flat** | Two people improvising with the same object. Let a fallen crate suggest a different approach, with a simple way to get it back into play. [Official game listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/477160/Human_Fall_Flat/) |
| **Flashing Lights** | Service work that players can understand by seeing who is at the wheel or beside the equipment. Give a partner a useful role in bringing the situation under control. |

Use those references to discuss a camera distance, a rhythm, or a feeling. Build the island’s characters, writing, materials, sounds, and places from its own fishing, farming, and harbor life.
