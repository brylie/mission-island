# Starter Island world-building brief

Build places that support a working day, tell part of the island’s story, and reward taking the long way home. Use the [biome plan](starter-island-biomes.md) to decide what belongs in a place and the [story and cadence brief](starter-island-story-and-cadence.md) to decide when a crew encounters it. Give the crew a route it can use from the first paid shift and recognize again as its responsibilities grow. The full island and its careers can grow from that example.

## Gathering supports local work

**Recommendation.** Keep small-scale gathering on Starter Island as an optional outing or a step in a job. A resident needs a particular material at a particular place. Players find it, work out how to carry or deliver it, and see what it helped change. Wren’s playable Materials checkout already does this with a marked branch near Meadow Slope Path, loose stone on the dry side of Splitstone Strand, and visible edging along the ferry approach. Use that sequence to teach players to identify, carry and use a material. The current wharf project receives timber hauled from Seabrook. [Materials checkout](../mechanics/repair-materials.md) · [Ferry terminal](../mechanics/ferry-terminal.md)

| Approach | What it gives the game | Decision for the first pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gather loose materials for a purpose | A light outing, a reason to read the landscape, and a visible contribution to a repair. | Build on the playable checkout. Give resources distinctive forms, believable locations, and a clear next use. |
| Use an axe or pickaxe as part of a job | A vehicle or tool can change how the crew tackles an obstruction or a load. A short sequence of inspection, processing, staging and hauling gives the driver, spotter and loader meaningful roles. | Prototype one job when a tool adds a decision. For example, cut a fallen branch into pieces that fit on the flatbed to reopen a roadside verge. Keep an ordinary solo route and a recovery path. A pickaxe fits a marked worksite sample or later quarry checkout. |
| Harvest standing trees and scenery rocks across the island | A renewable resource economy with room for repeated collecting and crafting. It also changes the scenery that players navigate and the shelter animals use. | Keep this out of the first world-building slice. Review again if players seek out repeated gathering and those outings lead to interesting work. Start any later extraction trial at a managed work site. |

Keep the island’s landmark trees, embedded boulders, habitat logs, and cover patches stable. Put collectible windfall or loose stone where terrain and conditions suggest it belongs, with a quiet cue while aiming or approaching. The playable checkout’s fixed source and refill are a convenient baseline for QA. Later, work sites can replenish on a visible schedule, and storm deposits can return at a small set of plausible places. If a future forestry job does fell a tree, use an assigned tree and a visible managed regrowth state. If a quarry job breaks rock, use a work face with a saved stock of material. Preserve coherent scenery, collision, and supply state for both players through reconnects and restarts. [Forestry and mining career #11](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/11)

## First build · Seabrook to Shellglass

Use the existing **Seabrook Market → Island Farm verge → Driftwood Beach → Shellglass and Splitstone** route. The crew can arrive during a paid shift, return for Mara’s outing, and recognize the same places when they need the loose stone for Wren. Start there before filling every habitat with detail. Use these authored anchors when laying out the route. The beach freight drop is (29, −24 m), the farm drop is (24, −17.2 m), the loose stone is (45.5, −27 m), and Wren’s edging is (−25.5, −17.6 m). The windfall source is a separate outing at (−27.9, 12.5 m). [Delivery positions](../../src/worlds/starter/layout/places.ts) · [Materials positions](../../src/worlds/starter/layout/sites.ts)

1. **Take baseline views.** Capture Market runoff, the farm and beach approach, and the tide staff from player height. Mark the sidewalk, load approaches, beach drop, passable tide-pool route, and stone pickup as clearances. Keep copies of the views on the same build.
2. **Shape the transitions.** Use shelter and use marks around the market, a planted or rough field edge at the farm, an open sandy strand, and a rocky transition toward Shellglass. Create wide changes players can read while walking and a few close details with a reason to be there. Keep the existing road alignment and check the loaded flatbed and barrow against it.
3. **Give the crew one outing to play.** Run the existing beach gear delivery. After hand-in, let the crew notice either the tide staff or Mara’s field clue and choose whether to follow it. On a later Materials checkout, see whether both players identify the loose stone and understand where it goes. Test its recognizability next to ordinary shore rocks before adjusting the pickup cue.
4. **Add one living detail once the route reads.** Keep the existing Driftwood fish school. Try one distinct plant grouping at the sheltered bank, or one shoreline bird behavior along wet sand and rock. Compare two weather or tide conditions and listen to what a player notices independently. Leave the regional species and resource lists available for later population passes.

Use [beach shaping #102](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/102), [Seabrook Market #113](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/113), and [reticle labels #111](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/111) for related world and interaction work. Preserve the Windstone landing meadow and flight envelope in the island-wide placement plan. [Biome clearance notes](starter-island-biomes.md#protect-routes-and-the-landing-meadow)

## Check what earns another outing

Run the existing delivery and Materials checkout through a disposable save or checkpoint. Walk the route both ways with labels hidden, then drive it with a loaded truck. Record where each person notices a habitat change, whether the pickup is easy to distinguish, and what they remember about the person who needed it. Note role swaps and time spent carrying out the job, waiting, or making a repeated trip. Ask what they would choose to do next. One run can split scouting and transport. A second player can repeat the route solo to check the same hand-in and recovery.

If the gathering step feels like walking to a marker and pressing **E**, first improve the clue, the place, or the purpose. If players enjoy reading the terrain, handling the material, and returning for new work, try the fallen-branch tool job as a second slice. Compare it with a simple pickup and see whether the tool creates a worthwhile role and a visible result. Record the human checks in [#52](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/52).
