# The island's deep history

**Proposed. Last tended 2026-11-23.** Most of the real-world background on this page is still unsourced; the list at the end and the [open questions](../open-questions.md#sources-to-find) say what each claim needs. Source ids in the text point to the [sources register](../sources.yaml).

Starter Island is older than anyone who works on it. Its shape, soils, plants and jobs all come from a long story: hard rock was lifted, ground down by ice, half drowned by the sea, then settled by forest, fire and people. This page tells that story in order. Each era lists what players can still see today and the habitat it shaped in the [biome plan](starter-island-biomes.md). The [island generation note](island-generation.md) uses the same story to set the terrain generator's parameters, so the land players walk on matches the history told here.

The story is original to Mission Island. Its processes follow real cool-temperate coasts of the kind described in the field guides the biome plan cites. The story is in the island register; the real processes behind each era are marked where they are sourced, and the rest are listed at the end as unsourced.

## 1. An old block of granite and sandstone

Long before any ice, a body of granite cooled deep underground beneath layers of sand that later hardened into sandstone. Slow uplift and erosion brought both to the surface. Granite is hard, resists weathering and breaks into rounded blocks along its joints ([granite uplands](../field-guide/places/granite-uplands.md#joints-why-granite-breaks-into-blocks)). Sandstone is softer, splits along its beds and wears into fins, clefts and pale sand. (The sandstone half is still unsourced.)

- **Still visible.** The bare crown of Windstone Ridge. The rock bands and tors on Cairn Summit and Fallow Ridge. The sandstone fins and Splitstone cleft at Driftwood. The Shellglass basin cut into a sandstone ledge.
- **Habitats.** Windstone Ridge, exposed coastal grass and rock, and the Shellglass and Splitstone rocky shore.

## 2. Ice carves the valleys

During the last cold stage, an ice cap covered the island. Glaciers flowed from the high granite toward the sea. A glacier flowing through an older V-shaped valley scours its sides and floor into a deep, broad trough (`os-mapzone-glaciation`). Where a small side glacier met a bigger one, its valley was left hanging above the main floor (that mechanism is still unsourced), and a waterfall into the main valley is a good sign of a hanging valley (`os-mapzone-glaciation`). When the ice melted, it dropped boulders it had carried far from their source, and left hollows that filled with water (still unsourced).

- **Still visible.**
  - Pine Draw and the Alder Creek valley, broad-floored with steep sides.
  - Fernfall, where the stream drops from a hanging valley onto the spring shelf.
  - Lone boulders on the meadows, sitting in the soil.
  - Mirror Pool in its ice-scoured basin.
  - Scree fans under the crags, where frost kept prising the rock apart.
- **Habitats.** The Fernfall stream corridor and the Mirror Pool wetland, plus Highwood's draws and creek.

**Divergence: scale.** Real glacial troughs and fjards are usually kilometres long (still unsourced). Starter Island is about 0.2 km², its highest point is Cairn Summit at about 80 m (see [pinned control points](island-generation.md)), and its two glacial valleys are carved 13–16 m deep (see [erosion](island-generation.md#erosion-turns-noise-into-land) and [the current bake](island-generation.md#the-current-bake)). The island compresses the landforms so a player can walk through all of them in one outing. Its model purpose is to **illustrate** what glaciers leave behind, not their size or how long they took. A player might wrongly learn that glacial valleys and fjords can be small; the [misconceptions](../misconceptions.md) page records that. The model also misses features real glaciated land has, such as moraines and the wide spread of erratics across a whole landscape.

## 3. The sea rises and drowns the valley mouths

As the ice sheets melted worldwide, the sea rose by around 120 metres. **Needs checking:** a search summary of `lambeck-2014-pnas` gives about 134 m at the glacial maximum, and the paper hasn't been opened yet (see [open questions](../open-questions.md#sources-to-find)). Close to a vanished ice sheet, the land itself springs back up once the ice is gone, and many glaciated coasts show raised beaches for that reason (still unsourced). This island lay near the ice's edge, where the crust rebounded less than the sea rose. So the sea flooded the lower ends of the glacial valleys and turned them into long, sheltered inlets, the kind called fjards where the land around them is low (still unsourced; `bird-2008` is recorded but unopened). The island's coastline became a pattern of drowned valleys between ridges of harder rock.

- **Still visible.** Harbor Cove, a drowned valley mouth deep enough for the ferry and sheltered enough for the dock. The coves on Highwood's west coast, where Alder Creek reaches the sea.
- **Habitats.** Harbor Cove and Depot, the nearshore shallows, and open coastal water.

## 4. Waves sort the coast

Once the sea settled near its present level, waves began their work. Hard granite headlands took the full force of the swell and were cut into cliffs and ledges. Between them, in the bays, the waves dropped sand worn from the sandstone. The wind blew the dry sand inland into dunes that grass began to hold. (This sorting is still unsourced; `komar-1998` is recorded but unopened.)

- **Still visible.** Windstone's sea-facing headland. Driftwood Beach and its dunes in the sheltered bay. The wrack line that every tide rearranges.
- **Habitats.** The Driftwood sandy strand, exposed coastal grass and rock, and the nearshore shallows.

## 5. Soil, then forest and heath

Bare ground left by the ice slowly became soil, and plants moved in: birch early, then Scots pine on the drier, well-drained granite slopes, with alder and willow by the streams and heather and bilberry on the thin, acid soils of the exposed shoulders (still unsourced here). Today's habitats, and the sources behind them, are on the [Highwood habitats](../field-guide/highwood-habitats.md) cards.

- **Still visible.** Pinewoods and the Highwood pine stands. The birch groves on Birch Flats. Juniper scrub in Juniper Hollow. Heather and crowberry on Windstone. The alder fringe along the creeks. Moss and rushes in the wet meadows.
- **Habitats.** Pinewoods, Juniper Hollow, the interior meadow, the Mirror Pool wetland, Windstone Ridge and Highwood's stands.

## 6. Fire and recovery, again and again

Fire has always been part of this landscape. Lightning, and later people, set dry grass and heath alight in long dry spells. After each fire the ground recovers in stages, from first regrowth to pioneers to trees, as [wildfire: after the fire](../field-guide/processes/wildfire.md#after-the-fire) describes. Charred standing trees can stay for decades as snags (still unsourced). So the forest is a patchwork of different ages, each patch recording a fire.

- **Still visible.** The burn scar at the Forest Lookout and its recovering verge. Snags and charred stumps in Highwood. Even-aged patches of young birch and pine where an old fire opened the canopy. Today the game keeps the scar of the most recent wildfire, and a new fire replaces it; scars that accumulate into a patchwork of ages are planned.
- **Habitats.** Pinewoods and the Highwood stands. The [Fire Watch](../mechanics/fire-watch.md) and the [Forest Lookout recovery](../forest-lookout-recovery.md) are the playable parts of this era.

## 7. People make the island a home

People came by sea and settled the sheltered south. They built the harbor in the drowned valley mouth and Seabrook on the gentle ground above it. They cleared the farm from the meadow and laid roads along the valley floors. Later they built the Fire Watch on Cairn Summit to protect the woods they depend on. Their paths still bend around old boulders, and their walls are built from the island's own granite and sandstone.

- **Still visible.** Harbor Depot and the dock, Seabrook and its market, Island Farm, the ferry ramp, the cottages, the roads and paths, the Fire Watch and the fire access road.
- **Habitats.** Harbor Cove and Depot, cottage lanes and gardens, Seabrook Village, and Island Farm and its field margins.

## Using the story

- **Places.** When a place needs a detail, ask which era made it. A boulder in a meadow is a glacial erratic and should sit in the soil, not on it. A cove should look like the mouth of a valley.
- **Jobs and discoveries.** Let the story give work and observations a reason: a scree fan that keeps blocking the fire road, a snag that is a woodpecker's home, a dune that needs marram planted to hold it.
- **Planned: an in-game field guide.** A field guide connected to the [field journal](../mechanics/field-journal.md) could explain species, habitats and ideas such as glacial valleys, fire succession and riparian strips. Players would unlock entries by observing them in the world, and this page's eras would supply its deep-history content. It is not built yet.
