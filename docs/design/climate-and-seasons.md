# Island climates and seasons

**Planned.** Starter Island belongs in the cool-temperate part of a fictional northern archipelago. The sea moderates the weather. Springs are cool, summers mild, autumns wet and windy, and winters damp with occasional frost and short-lived snow. Sheltered gardens and woodland can support broadleaf trees. Exposed ridges favor hardier plants and wind-shaped conifers. The chimneys make sense on a cold morning, and long summer evenings give the crew time to linger at the harbor. Keep exact latitude and calendar length open until we test how time feels in play.

## From climate to a place players recognize

An island's climate sets the typical daylight, temperature, rainfall and prevailing winds through the year. The season shifts those tendencies. Daily weather plays out within them. Within the same island, drainage, elevation, shelter, sun exposure and salt spray explain why neighboring habitats differ.

| Place | Local conditions | What the player should see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Driftwood Beach | Salt spray, freely draining sand and exposed wind | Low grasses gather in sheltered pockets. Bare sand and wrack mark the reach of the surf. A stable path leads back from the beach in every season. |
| Pinewoods and Windstone Ridge | Woodland shelter gives way to thinner soil and stronger wind | Upright trees stand inside the wood. Near the ridge, crowns lean and flag in a consistent direction. Grass and low scrub fill gaps toward the exposed slope. |
| Mirror Pool | Fresh water, damp soil and sheltered margins | Reeds and sedges follow the wet edge. Drier ground grades into grasses and shrubs. Seasonal flower and wildlife activity changes within the same recognizable wetland. |
| Seabrook and the cottages | Sheltered gardens near a working harbor | Garden plants and deciduous street trees mark the time of year. Roofs, chimneys, garden beds and market displays show how residents live through it. |

Define a climate profile for each island when it needs one. An island with a longer, warmer growing season could support different crops and coastal plants. One with greater exposure could support salt-tolerant vegetation and give players more reason to seek sheltered landings. Saltwind can feel distinct through wind and shelter even if the crossing stays within the same climate. Choose its long-term profile from its existing coast, river valley and forests when that affects a playable trip. Use local gradients to place vegetation and connect habitats. A climate can support several biomes, and the same biome family can have different species and seasonal behavior on two islands. [Saltwind site inventory](../saltwind-island.md)

## Four seasons on Starter Island

| Season | Cues along a familiar route | A reason to return |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spring | New leaf buds, fresh reeds and wildflowers in sheltered places | Visit Mirror Pool to record the first blooms in the field journal. |
| Summer | Full crowns, flowering meadows and longer evenings | Take an evening fishing outing or stay for a market gathering. |
| Autumn | Turning foliage, seed heads, fallen leaves and windy spells | Bring in a harvest and help prepare Seabrook's harvest market. |
| Winter | Bare deciduous crowns, greener conifers, low light and occasional frost on sheltered ground | See cottage windows and chimney smoke on the way to a small harbor lantern evening. |

These are opportunities for revisiting known places. Keep basic earnings, the first favors and ferry progress viable throughout the year. List upcoming gatherings on a noticeboard with a clear in-game date. Give a crew that misses one another chance at the next occurrence or an ordinary path to any essential reward. Let seasonal fishing, farming and festivals grow from this calendar after it works in play.

The host owns and saves one calendar for the crew. Both browsers derive the same date and local season from it, including after sleep, a late join, travel or restart. Sleeping advances the calendar to the next dawn. Weather remains local to each island, and tides retain their own cycle. For older saves, choose a default starting date that preserves the familiar scenery and existing progress. Keep plant positions and procedural seeds stable as growth, foliage and activity change. Preserve paths, landmarks, accepted jobs and discoveries through transitions.

## First playable slice

1. In the Landscape Workbench, compare summer and late autumn at Pinewoods from one saved view. Hold camera, seed, hour and weather steady. Change the foliage stage and undergrowth of established plants. Then walk the same path in both states to check sightlines and collision. Record a saved setup and matched captures once the season control exists.
2. Drive that narrow preview through the shared calendar in a disposable Starter Island save. Set up a checkpoint just before a boundary. In two browsers, sleep through it, join after dawn and restart the host. Confirm the date, foliage and routes agree, and that a job accepted before the change is still completable.
3. Add one return visit with a concrete payoff, such as a seasonal journal sighting or a recurring Seabrook market gathering. Watch whether crews can tell what changed, find the event and keep working toward their usual goals. Use that to tune how long a season should last and how much change to show in the first few sessions.
