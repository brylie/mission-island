# Design decisions and open questions

Record a choice here when another designer could reasonably make the opposite choice while working on a different system. Include the reason, the consequence a player can notice, and the evidence that would make the team revisit it. GitHub issues hold the implementation work.

## Established direction

| Choice | Reason and consequence | Where to follow it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two players can join from separate browsers on the LAN. | A parent and child can build and play together with one host laptop. A client refresh should be enough to try an edit. | [Running the game](../../README.md#play-on-your-local-network) |
| Vehicle exteriors support a third-person driving view. | The crew can see a partner approaching, cargo shifting, and the space around the truck. Check the same proportions on foot and from above. | [Game design](../game-design.md#feel-and-art-direction) |
| One crew bank follows the players across islands. Planned careers and trust are shared. | A market sale or a completed job advances the same goal even when the players work apart. Either player can spend crew coins. | [Progression and ownership](progression.md#what-belongs-to-whom) |
| Every job should be doable solo and easier together through distinct roles. | A solo hauler can stage, load and drive. In a pair, one stages freight while the other positions the truck. Playtest both ways and look for useful work for each role. | [Crew orders playtest](../mechanics/crew-orders.md#playtest) |
| Restore a dedicated ferry terminal at the end of Harbor Depot road. | The repair gives several livelihoods a shared purpose. The small fishing dock keeps its use for the skiff and fishing. | [Community project](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/4) |
| A resident lends a shared toolbox for a favor rather than adding a tool to a personal inventory. | The loaner is a world item either player can carry, set down or hand over at the job. If a carrier leaves, the partner asks the resident to put it back on the mending bench and carries on. Revisit if players expect a borrowed tool in their own inventory, or if a later favor needs a tool both players use at once. | [Neighbor repairs](../mechanics/neighbor-repairs.md) |
| One host-owned loaner wing serves the ridge, and every touchdown returns it to the rack. | Flying stays free and there is never a second wing to recover after a missed landing, a disconnect or a restart. Only one player flies at a time; the other spots or meets the pilot. Revisit when the crew can earn a wing or a fabric choice of its own. | [Ridge gliding](../mechanics/ridge-gliding.md) |
| A favor credits once per favor id in a shared ledger, and a resident's schedule never gates an existing service. | Either crewmate can agree and either can finish, so the pair can split the work and a late joiner sees the same record. A repeated greeting or hand-in earns nothing. Residents come and go, but the market, the clothing counter and the job board always work, and a resident's prompt never takes **E** from them. Revisit when a favor needs a resident's personal presence or a breach can cost trust. | [Residents and favors](../mechanics/resident-favors.md) |
| Trust is lost only through a knowing false report, never by accident. | A failed attempt, a disconnect or a declined favor is a chance to try again. A breach needs a finding both players can see, and the conversation shows that finding beside both answers. A shared finding means one player can lie about what the other saw. That is part of being a crew: both hear about it afterward. Revisit if players breach without understanding the stakes. | [Residents and favors](../mechanics/resident-favors.md#reporting-back-and-broken-promises) |
| Small talk is chosen on each client from conditions on the shared snapshot. | Remarks are flavor, so the host doesn't need to agree on them, and each crewmate can hear something different. Conditions keep them truthful to the shared world. A remark that changes progress or reveals a clue belongs in the resident's conversation lines instead. | [Small talk](../mechanics/resident-favors.md#small-talk) |
| The ferry tally board, not a resident, introduces the repair and records it. | It shows the stage, each crewmate's coins and timber, and the next step, even when Bram, the harbor steward, is off duty. Bram can later introduce the work at the board and greet the crew by the stages they finished. | [Ferry terminal](../mechanics/ferry-terminal.md) |
| Ferry repair stages take crew coins and timber hauled from Seabrook. | The two together combine earning with a delivery, so crews that fish and farm and crews that haul both reach the repair. The timber rides in the depot crates, so delivery orders wait while a haul is on the way. The crewmate who stacks a crate is credited with it. The stage costs are trial values. | [Ferry terminal](../mechanics/ferry-terminal.md) · [Balance #52](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/52) |
| Restoring the terminal opens the ramp but does not yet start crossings. | The save records when service was restored, so the crossing in [#20](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/20) can build on it. Whether the crossing is continuous travel or loads a second map stays open. | [Ferry terminal](../mechanics/ferry-terminal.md) |
| Keep the landscape familiar and tie material gathering to specific work. | Players can navigate by landmarks they know. Story milestones, skills, relationships, and seasons give them new things to do when they return. | [Familiar places and changing opportunities](../game-design.md#familiar-places-and-changing-opportunities) |
| Generate variation around recognizable places. | A resident can give reliable directions even as weather, animal behavior, or vegetation changes. Use reproducible conditions to review a change to traversal or shared progress. | [Generativity](../game-design.md#generativity) |
| Highwood trees are built from 3 to 9 faceted clumps per crown and judged by silhouette and greyscale value before colour. | Trees fill most of a backcountry frame, so forms must survive distance and flat overcast light. Each species keeps its own value band: dark pine, mid alder, light birch, silvery willow. A far tier merges limb groups to keep each crown's extent, and swaps in beyond 100 m. Revisit if the swap is visible in the game camera. **The 100 m switch is superseded** by the two-tier forest row below (80 m with a 16 m dead band). | [How the generator is built](../highwood-species.md#how-the-generator-is-built) · [Highwood plants](../field-guide/highwood-plants.md) |
| A far Highwood tree may use up to 160 triangles, not 120. | At 120, single-ring masses read as diamonds and popped at the tier switch; two rings fix it and still cost about a sixth of the near tree. Shrubs and deadwood keep far budgets of 24–120. Revisit if instanced forests cost too much frame time. | `templateBudgets` in [`tree-species.ts`](../../src/assets/geometry/tree-species.ts) |
| Starter Island is cool-temperate and maritime. | Wind, shelter and drainage explain changes between habitats. Four shared seasons give familiar places reasons to revisit. The calendar pace and first seasonal outing need a playtest. | [Island climates and seasons](climate-and-seasons.md) |
| Generate Starter Island's north-west as land first and let the coast emerge from it. | A baked, hash-checked height field replaces the per-bearing coast radius there, so Highwood gets valleys, coves, straits and islets rather than a smooth blob. `coastPoint` stays a radius per bearing, giving the farthest shore along each ray for bounds and buoys, while the true shoreline is traced at load by marching squares for the surf and the map. Revisit if the load-time trace grows too slow, or if the baked asset outgrows the 500 KB pre-commit file limit (it is 454 KB now). | [Island generation](island-generation.md) |
| Highwood trees get no physics bodies. Walking reads a 4 m grid of trunks, snags and logs instead. | Each static body raises the cost of every physics step, and the forest has thousands of plants. A walking check reads the few cells around a walker. Vehicles don't meet Highwood trunks yet: Phase 3 adds trunk colliders only along the fire road and tracks. Revisit if vehicles need to meet trees off-road. | [Highwood backcountry](../backcountry.md#performance) · [Physics cost](../architecture.md#physics-cost-and-static-colliders) |
| The Highwood forest draws in two tiers: near thin instances and far merged meshes, one per 64 m tile and material. A tile switches at 80 m from the camera to its nearest point, with a 16 m dead band. | Thin instances keep near trees to one batch per template, and far tiles cull whole. Measuring to a tile's nearest point means a corner close to the camera counts as near, and the dead band stops tiles flickering between tiers. This supersedes the 100 m Workbench switch above. Revisit if the switch shows in the game camera. | [Rendering and detail](../craft/rendering-and-detail.md#hiding-the-switch) · [Highwood forest](../architecture.md#highwood-forest) |
| Highwood's planting is baked at generation time, not classified at load. | Decoding the bake takes about 6 ms; classifying habitats and placing plants at load took about 180 ms. A source-hash test catches a stale bake. Revisit if the bake outgrows the 500 KB pre-commit file limit, or if plants must change at runtime. | [Highwood forest](../architecture.md#highwood-forest) · [Highwood backcountry](../backcountry.md#forest) |
| Graphics detail has Automatic, High and Low. Automatic starts at High and drops to Low once per session, and never climbs back. | A picture that keeps changing is worse than a steady lower one. The 40 ms threshold sits clear of a 30 Hz cap (33.3 ms frames), so a capped browser stays on High. Revisit after the playtest on an older laptop. | [Player guide](../player-guide.md#controls) · [Highwood backcountry](../backcountry.md#performance) |
| A scene-wide fog plugin replaces Babylon's fog on every standard and PBR material. | Babylon mixes fog before image processing, so tone mapping and contrast hazed materials differently: PBR ground washed pale while standard trees and walls hazed toward another colour than the sky. The plugin mixes after image processing, so things at the same distance fade alike. There is no per-material opt-out yet. | [Scene fog contract](../architecture.md#highwood-forest) · [#219](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/219) |
| Roads cut and fill the island at runtime as a layer over the natural ground, not in the terrain bake. | Physics, wheels, walking, the map and footsteps all read `groundHeight`, so a road there reaches every system at once. Keeping roads out of the terrain bake means a road can change without re-baking the land; only the planting is re-baked. The grading costs about 170 ms once at load. Revisit if more roads make that too slow on older hardware. | [Graded roads](../architecture.md#graded-roads) |
| Roads are graded in list order, each over the ground the earlier ones shaped. | A branch then starts level with the road it leaves, and an overlap is cut once rather than twice. A branch must come after its road in the layout. | [Graded roads](../architecture.md#graded-roads) · [Design roads](../workbench.md#design-roads) |
| Roads are realistic and efficient: straight where a straight road holds the grade, curving only along contours or in switchbacks. | Real roads are built for cost and use, not for driving thrills, and wiggles on level ground read as artificial. Suggest route treats grade under the limit as nearly free and pulls its path straight wherever it can. | [Forest roads](../field-guide/practices/forest-roads.md) · [Design roads](../workbench.md#design-roads) |
| The Workbench saves road layouts straight into the source tree, through a development-server endpoint that writes one file. | Designers edit roads on the map and see them in 3D, and the layout lands as a reviewable source diff rather than a downloaded file to copy across. The endpoint accepts only loopback requests with a custom header a web page cannot send without a refused preflight. | [Workbench save endpoint](../architecture.md#graded-roads) |
| The Cairn Summit spur ends at a pad about 31 m up, below the crown, and allows 15%. | Cairn's top 10 m is too steep for any road, as at many real lookouts, where the road ends at a parking area and the last climb is on foot. 15% on a short spur is within what the flatbed, pickup and tractor climb. Revisit with the Phase 4 Fire Watch. | [The fire road](../backcountry.md#fire-road) |

## Questions with a way to answer them

| Open question | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- |
| How much work should ferry repair take? | Run both earning routes, record active minutes, island days and purchases, and ask when the next contribution became clear. [Balance playtest](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/52) |
| Should the crossing stream the next island or load it separately? | Carry a tagged crate across, reconnect a passenger, and return with the right cargo, order and crew bank balance. Use travel time and host performance alongside that result. [Ferry check-in](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/20) |
| How long should a season last, and which seasonal outing should come first? | Compare Pinewoods in summer and late autumn, then watch a crew return for one journal sighting or market gathering. Note how many sessions it takes to notice a change and whether the crew can still finish regular work. [First playable slice](climate-and-seasons.md#first-playable-slice) |

For a new idea, capture the **moment**, the **player choice**, and the **smallest test**. For example, a fisher mentioning shelter after rain could prompt the crew to take a woodland detour. Try one line of dialogue and one observable hare behavior, then ask whether the players found the clearing and could explain what they saw. Link a chosen experiment from the [roadmap](../roadmap.md) or its milestone. When a playtest settles a question, update the affected spec and record the reasoning here.
