# Starter Island biome plan

**Proposed, reviewed against main at `e521ac2`.** Starter Island should sustain a whole evening of play. The crew can earn from local work, take a different route home, discover something about the island, and have a reason to come back. Give every reachable stretch of ground and water a habitat identity, including gardens, streets, working yards and farm margins. The first pass establishes the areas and their edges. Plant and animal populations follow once the island reads as a whole.

### Connect habitats through work and wildlife

- Reuse existing routes first. The market and dock give gathered goods a destination. Wren’s materials job supplies a model for marked, loose samples. The journal already gives a reason to visit a place and return to catch a different behavior. [Resident favors](../mechanics/resident-favors.md) · [Repair materials](../mechanics/repair-materials.md) · [Field journal](../mechanics/field-journal.md)
- Give resources a reason to be available. Crops and cultivated cuttings come from tended plots. Storm finds appear at plausible deposition sites. Use designated loose materials for collection and keep living plants, occupied shells, shelter logs and other habitat features available for observation.
- Let animals belong to the island across habitat edges. One gull can feed at Shellglass, circle Windstone lift and roost on an outer ledge. The meadow forage outside Juniper can support the same hares that shelter in the hollow. Use these routes and behaviors as opportunities for the crew to observe, rather than adding a separate flock or population for every row. [Gull behavior #105](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/105)
- Treat the errands as a pool of opportunities. The [world-building brief](starter-island-worldbuilding-brief.md) scopes the first route and where gathering and work tools fit. Start with one route that joins work and observation, such as a farm delivery followed by a Driftwood or Shellglass note. Add one event at a time and use the [activity scheduler #148](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/148) to keep urgent incidents and resident requests spaced out.

## A day worth spending here

- **A working day with a detour.** Take a board delivery from the depot to the farm or beach, or grow and sell a Seabrook order. Can the crew finish the work and spontaneously choose a second trip or stop somewhere on the way home? The village, fields, and shore should feel different at walking speed.
- **An outing with a reason to return.** Take the beach route to Shellglass, or walk from woodland cover to Mirror Pool. Use the existing field notebook to record something the crew observes. Return another hour or in different weather to see a change. Preserve per-player discovery labels and the existing shared notebook and credit.
- **A longer local outing.** Choose to fly an aerial survey, visit a neighbor, or help with the ferry project between paying jobs. Keep those entrances readable and leave ordinary fishing and a quiet visit to the beach available after ferry restoration. The island should still offer a next day the crew would choose.

## Implement and review in passes

1. **Classify the whole island.** Use the current shoreline, depth, drainage, slope and exposure, plus authored areas for managed land. Give each accessible point a primary identity for the map and blend neighboring habitats for scenery. Use continuous gradients, with seeded variation at the scale of a bank or clearing. Check coverage across both land and navigable water. Preserve existing discovery IDs and use a Workbench-only overlay for the first review.
2. **Make one continuous route legible.** Start with Seabrook → farm verge → Driftwood → Shellglass. Use ground cover, openness, shelter and shore material to carry the transitions. Walk it both ways with labels hidden and ask each player where the character changed. Then review cottages → Pinewoods → Juniper, Fernfall → Mirror Pool → Windstone, and a skiff outing from Harbor Cove to Driftwood. Capture matched Island and eye-level views. Repeat the landing and work-route checks before adding dense scenery.
3. **Populate the areas.** Audit what the game already uses. For each area, start with one representative plant grouping and one wildlife behavior a player can observe at walking distance. Use shelter, water, forage and routes to decide where populations belong. Let an animal cross between suitable habitats during its day. Review populations and interactions in Biome Composer, then confirm them in the field with both players. Add seasonal variations after the underlying place reads clearly.
