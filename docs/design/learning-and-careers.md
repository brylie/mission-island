# Learning through island work

**Design direction.** Mission Island can help children and adults learn practical skills by doing useful work together. A resident demonstrates a technique because they need something done. Players try it with room to make mistakes, see what their choices changed, then encounter a different task where the same idea helps. An experienced player can take the lead one day and learn a new role from their partner the next.

## How a career grows

1. **Notice.** Use a real cue in the island. A bobber dips, a leaf droops, a load shifts, or a stuck hinge pulls the gate out of line. A resident can demonstrate what to watch for while working.
2. **Try with support.** Give the crew a small useful job and enough time to think. Let players ask for a hint, look at a demonstration again, or recover from a mistake.
3. **Apply the idea somewhere else.** Change the cargo, route, work site, weather, or responsibility. The player should need to recognize the same underlying idea in the new situation.
4. **Take on more responsibility.** A checkout can open equipment and jobs once the crew shows readiness in play. Crew qualifications open access together. Both players should be able to revisit practice and learn a role they did not perform the first time.

Teach the underlying decisions the game can show faithfully, such as checking the result of a repair, making space for a load, and comparing observations made under similar conditions. Keep terminology in the scene where it helps. Offer an optional journal or resident explanation when someone wants the real-world connection. When a mechanic compresses time or simplifies a technique, describe that simplification in the relevant learning brief.

## Possible pathways from Starter Island

| Playable starting point | Skill to practise | Proposed job that builds on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wren's sagging gate | Observe the symptom, identify a likely cause, fit a replacement and test that it worked. | Check another misaligned fixture. One player operates it while the other watches where it catches. Later work can introduce workshop maintenance and inspection. |
| Harbor Depot delivery | Plan the approach, place a load and check it before driving. | At a different bay, use a spotter and learn why load position and restraints matter before larger freight work. [First hauling qualification #50](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/50) |
| Fishing and farming | Read a cue and choose when to act. A bite calls for a response. A dry plot needs water. | Compare two fishing spots or check sheltered and exposed plots. Use observations to plan the next outing or crop. |
| Watching wildlife at Driftwood or Juniper Hollow | Describe what an animal did and record the place, time, and conditions. | The [field journal](../mechanics/field-journal.md) notes the first record. Later, compare repeated observations from shore and from a wading approach. Later a resident researcher could ask the crew to survey two sites and decide what the records support. [Field journal #8](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/8) · [First field outing #51](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/51) |

Research can become a career built from ordinary curiosity. Start by recording a sighting with its place, time and conditions. Move to repeating the same watch under comparable conditions, then to finding an explanation and recommending an action. For a first survey, one player could watch from shore and log when the fish scatter while their partner takes the same route through the water. Swap roles. A later job could ask where to place a marker that keeps skiffs clear of a shallow habitat. Give the evidence a purpose in the island.

## First learning playtest

Use Wren’s playable gate repair. Set a familiar goal and watch how a first-time player finds the cause, what tells them the test succeeded, and whether their partner could explain it from what they saw. Use a fresh visit for the first attempt and the existing fit checkpoint to repeat the last steps with roles swapped. The next proposed trial is a second fixture with a different symptom so players can apply the inspect, repair, and test sequence with fewer hints.

For each career brief, record what players can practise, how the task shows cause and effect, and where the skill becomes useful again.
