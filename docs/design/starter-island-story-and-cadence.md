# The first weeks on Starter Island

**Proposed pacing for the fresh-save playtest.** Starter Island is the crew’s home for a season or two. Let the players learn to earn a living, come to know their neighbors, and return to familiar habitats before they finish restoring the ferry wharf. Aim for the wharf to open in island week three or four. The first playable test is whether those weeks hold work and outings the crew would freely choose.

## What the build already does

After four completed board orders, the tally board opens the ferry project. Bracing the landing, laying the deck, and opening the ramp each take 12 crew coins and two timber crates from Seabrook. Stages finish on the final contribution. The rebuilt ramp can already be walked and driven on. The travel handoff is tracked in #20. Use this playable sequence as the baseline for the story and pacing changes. ## Story and chapter beats

The damaged landing is visible on arrival. Bram can explain what the lost service means to his neighbors and point toward the closed ramp. Players first have their own reasons to be out on the island. Later, several of those people, routes, and skills come together in the rebuild.

| Island time | Immediate focus | What opens or changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Week 1 · Find your feet** | Learn the route from the cottages to a first paid shift. Buy or grow something useful, take a small job by choice, and keep a promise to one resident. Wren’s gate is an example. | First earnings and ordinary work are available early. A later greeting, Wren’s shortcut, or a resident’s field clue shows that the crew already matters here. Bram explains the wharf and what will eventually make it usable. |
| **Week 2 · Become useful** | Pick a line of work to deepen. A hauling crew can learn to position and load safely. A crew that prefers the farm and skiff can fill market orders and learn the supply run. Fit a Driftwood or Mirror Pool visit between jobs. | One local checkout or handoff earns a useful responsibility. The tally board opens a tangible first repair once the crew has shown it can bring supplies. On a later visit the repaired supports are visible and the next work is clear. |
| **Weeks 3–4 · Finish a community project** | Bring the deck materials and help keep the road and yard usable. Spread the project across a few days with room for other paid work or a visit home. For the last outing, approach the ramp with a load, check the landing, and see the gantry lamps come on. | The deck and ramp become visibly usable. Neighbors acknowledge what the crew did. A small opening-day errand or gathering can mark the finish, and the first crossing becomes a trip the crew can choose. |
| **After opening · Stay another season** | Do a local return load, take a more skilled job, follow an unfinished journal clue, or come back to a familiar habitat in another season. | The restored wharf supports ordinary island work. Give each crew a satisfying morning at home after the opening and something worth coming back for in the next season. |

## What should hold back the wharf

Use a few achievements that make sense to the people depending on the ramp. Test these as shared crew milestones.

- **Earn and choose work.** The crew gets its first shared pay, and later completes a job it chose. Use the board or a meaningful local order to establish that it can bring in supplies.
- **Keep a local commitment.** Let one finished favor introduce the crew to the project. Offer a choice of residents whose circumstances connect to the harbor. Wren can show what completing a repair looks like. Ines or Bram can explain why dependable deliveries matter.
- **Prove the supply route and do the physical work.** Try counting different local achievements toward readiness for the first stage. A crew can demonstrate that it can deliver with the flatbed, or fill a substantial provision order and follow up with a short timber run. Both routes should show that the crew can stage supplies at the yard. Inspect the finished approach and walk or drive onto the restored ramp during the opening.

Use the current four-order rule as the comparison. If a more meaningful readiness rule replaces it, credit paid orders and any repair already made in older saves. Keep career specializations, field journal completion, and optional incidents available as reasons to play longer. Time a seasonal return visit around a change players can see, such as turning leaves followed by a market gathering a few days later. A crop failure, a passed-over call, or an early purchase should leave another reasonable route to the wharf. A quiet player can contribute by fishing or growing food while the other hauls.

## What one island day feels like

- **Morning.** A familiar resident, the job board, or the tally board gives the crew a next step. Let the shared activity scheduler keep one goal prominent and leave other commitments in the crew guide. A day can stay quiet.
- **During work.** After a short job or visit, leave a natural point to split up or take a detour. Give the player a reason to notice the place, such as the fields opening toward Driftwood or a tide staff that reads differently on the way back.
- **Evening.** Show a small durable outcome. Both players see a paid order, a filled place in the tally, a patched path, or a new notebook entry. They can choose to stay out or turn in and see what changes the next day.

Use any overnight construction or handoff to show a visible next step the crew can understand. Start with varied contributions and distinct work across the stages. If early runs finish in a few repetitive orders, try changing the work and what players learn before increasing the price. If players spend a whole day waiting, give them a shorter next step or a useful plan for that day. ## Playtest the pace together

Interpret weeks as **island weeks**. At the current eight-minute day, three to four weeks of uninterrupted clock time are about 2 h 48 min to 3 h 44 min. Sleeping shortens that considerably. Record active minutes and completed outings separately from the calendar so skipped nights cannot make an empty schedule look like a full season. For the first seasonal comparison, try a season of roughly two to three island weeks. The season length and opening date remain playtest targets. [Island clock](../../src/weather.ts) · [Climate and seasons](climate-and-seasons.md)

Use one fresh two-player save that leans on deliveries and a second that leans on farming and fishing. Start together, spend some crew coins on ordinary needs, spend one day on a local outing, and try one meaningful role swap. Record the first payday, first kept favor, first checkout or supply run, each visible repair, and one thing each player chose to do after opening. Record active crew minutes, island days, skipped nights, repeated orders, idle waits, and how often both players felt pulled off their plans. Check a solo route and load an existing save before changing qualification rules.

At the end, ask each player why the wharf is open now, what they helped make possible, and what they would do tomorrow if they stayed. Use their answers and measured sessions to tune the season length, contribution amounts, and opening cadence in [#52](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/52).
