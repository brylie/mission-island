# Harbor gull landings

The Starter dock flock uses five of the existing piling caps. The Saltwind flock uses four spots along the west edge of the Brinewick quay. Each gull has its own perch, departure time, and flight path. An incoming gull spreads its wings in flight, lowers its feet during the flare, and folds its wings once planted. Heavy rain and dusk shorten the circuit and give the bird more time on the perch. At night the birds stay perched.

In live play, a gull reads the shared weather at the start of its circuit. A shower that starts while it is airborne affects the next circuit. The older general-purpose flock renderer still handles inland birds and bats.

## Regression checks

At wet noon and 5 seconds the old path applied a 90% rest pose in midair. To reproduce it, serve baseline `df12e9f` and open [the original roost-facing shot](http://localhost:5173/workbench.html?view=1&focus=-7.7%2C28&target=-7.7%2C5.25%2C28&orbit=-1.77%2C1.31%2C9.1%2C0.72&sky=12%2C1%2C5&display=0%2C0#landscape). Press **Play** to see the wings hanging down while the gull still translates. Open the same shot on this branch to verify the old animation is gone, then use the dock links to see the birds land on their new supports.

The deterministic tests check heading and motion across trajectory joins, stationary feet through settling, exposed wings in flight, staggered departures, both sets of perch supports, and a live weather change during a circuit. The matched wet-noon clips cover 5–12 seconds in both builds from the original roost-facing camera. A separate clip covers 35.2–42.2 seconds at the updated dock in steady rain. An independent viewer’s read of the motion at full speed remains to be recorded.
