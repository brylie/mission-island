## Flying and birds

| Report | Checked on main and next step |
| --- | --- |
| **The hang glider is hard to sustain and feels unrewarding.** | The merged checkout [#94](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/94) and guided soaring runs establish that routes exist. The earlier Juniper run used an autopilot reading game snapshots. Run `ridge-soaring` in clear daylight with an uncoached pilot and a ground observer. Ask the pilot to find lift, gain enough height to leave it, reach a second landmark, and return or land safely. Record route, height, time aloft and where they lost lift. Compare wider or gentler training lift, glide ratio and steering only after seeing where pilots struggle. [Glider handling #145](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/145) tracks this human flight check and the lift-mote review. | **Lift effects are widespread and some appear to descend. Visibility needs testing.** | The renderer draws lift motes from weather even without an airborne pilot. The particle phase multiplies elapsed time by current strength, so a falling strength can move the phase backward. This is a code lead to test at changed weather and long elapsed times. Keep nearby motes subtle for players on foot and fade distant columns by distance. Give the pilot a clearer view of working lift in flight. Only the mote visibility should depend on the viewer’s flight state. Keep circling gulls visible as a natural clue to lift. From the same area, compare the pilot and ground spotter views, then land and check that distant motes fade smoothly while the birds continue circling. [Lift renderer](../../src/ridge-aviation-view.ts) · [Mote phase](../../src/assets/ridge-updraft.ts) |
| **Gulls and other birds should visit more places and have their own perch and roost routines.** | Dock perches come from harbor piles. Thermal gulls are rendered separately at lift sites. Keep the thermals as one stop in a gull’s day. [Gull behavior #105](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/105) now calls for a test that follows an individual out of a thermal along a continuous flight to another site, such as a shoreline feeding area, a perch or a roost. Let departure timing and destinations vary by bird and conditions. Watch from the ground and from the air to see whether the bird’s route continues through the transition. Use [#65](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/65) for takeoff and landing poses and [#60](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/60) for other species and distinct perch choices.
