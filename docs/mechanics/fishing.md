# Fishing and cast timing

Taking the starter toolkit from your home chest equips the rod. While carrying the toolkit, press **1** to select the rod again. Stop at any accessible shoreline, on the harbor dock, or aboard a settled skiff. Aim over open water with the follow camera. In island view, face the water before charging.

1. Hold **left click** to start the cast meter. It rises for 0.88 seconds, falls for 0.88 seconds, and repeats.
2. Release near the top for the longest cast and the best odds of a fish. The cast travels 3.1 to 12.3 meters. It needs at least 0.14 meters of water at the landing point.
3. Watch the bobber. After the 0.42-second cast and a 3.1-second wait, a successful bite starts the reeling challenge.
4. Hold **left click** while the fish is calm to reel it in. Release when it pulls to let the line go slack. Full progress lands one fish. Full tension snaps the line, and the fish escapes if the fight lasts 23 seconds. Sell landed fish at Seabrook Market for 2 coins each.

The host computes power and resolves each player's bite and fight independently. Bite probability is 22% at minimum power and 98% at full power, with a quadratic increase. Moving or swapping tools retrieves the line. Paddling or steering the skiff interrupts both lines. On the dock, aim beside or beyond the deck into open water.

## Playtest

- Compare release during the upswing, at the peak, and during the downswing.
- Cast from dry shore, the end of the dock, and a stationary skiff. Aim at land to check the dry-cast prompt.
- Land a fish by reeling during calm periods. Keep reeling through a pull to test snapping the line, then wait out a fight to test an escape. Only landing the fish adds it to inventory.
- Cast from both skiff seats, then paddle with one player. Both bobbers should disappear.
- Sell the catch and check that the fish leave the inventory and both players see the sale credited once to the crew bank.

## First catch at the dock · proposed trial

Asked where fishing first became confusing, a player answered “Understanding why the fish escaped.” We did not record their inputs. In the current game, ask the angler what they saw, what happened and what they will try next. Compare their answer with the actual outcome: a cast without a bite, a line snapped by tension, or a hooked fish escaping when the fight runs out of time. Record the next cast as well as the explanation. [Mara and the fishing failure playtest](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/67)

Try a simpler encounter for a common starter fish. Give the first valid cast a bite so a newcomer can learn the cue. Let the float bob gently while the fish investigates. When it dips fully under, show “Click to reel in” beside the splash. One click during the dip lands the fish. Try a three-second window initially. An early click leaves the float in place. On a missed dip, let the float resurface and say “It let go. Click when the float goes under.” Give a quiet cast or a line retrieved by a moving skiff its own explanation. Keep the cue readable with sound muted. Mara’s proposed demonstration should use the same dip and rod lift.

At the small dock, ask a newcomer to say “now” when they would reel, then try their own rod while their partner watches quietly. Record whether they recognized the dip, whether they landed a fish within two bites, and the first hint they needed. After a deliberate miss, ask what they will change before explaining the cue. Repeat from the stationary skiff with one angler landing a fish and the other missing. Check that each sees the reason for their own outcome and that only the landed fish enters its angler’s inventory. Tune the beginner window from these observations before deciding whether fishing experience should extend it. [Shared playtest record](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/52)
