# Misconceptions

**Status: started. Last tended 2026-11-23.**

Ideas people commonly hold that aren't true, and whether the island makes each one better or worse. Divergence notes say where the game departs from reality. This page gathers the departures most likely to teach something wrong, and how we counter them. It's for designers checking a mechanic, and teachers planning a debrief after play.

Each row: the misconception, what is true, how the island might reinforce it, and how we counter it. *What is true* links to the explainer that holds its sources, and those claims are still being sourced. *Where it comes from* and *Sources* are left out until checked. Don't fill them from memory.

| Misconception | What is true | How the island could reinforce it | How we counter it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A dry spell starts wildfires. | Something must ignite a fire. Weather makes ignition and spread more or less likely. | Island fires come sooner after dry weather, and their ignition is never shown. | The [wildfire explainer](field-guide/processes/wildfire.md#where-the-island-differs) says so. A resident or journal line could name a cause, such as a discarded match. |
| Slope doesn't affect how fire spreads. | Fire runs faster uphill. | Island fires spread between hotspots without regard to slope. | The wildfire divergence table. A future spread rule could favour uphill hotspots. |
| One tower gives a fire's exact location. | One tower gives a bearing and a rough distance; crossing bearings from two or more towers fixes the position precisely. | The island's fire finder gives place, bearing and distance from one tower, and one binocular mark fixes a spot exactly, with no cross-check. | The wildfire divergence table. A second tower could teach triangulation. |
| Extinguishers are the main tool against wildfires. | Beaters, firebreaks and water from crews matter more on open moorland. | The crew fights every fire with extinguishers. | The wildfire divergence table. |
| A rangefinder reads straight through leaves. | Part of a laser beam can return from leaves or branches in front of the target, and rangefinders offer closest, farthest or strongest-return modes to deal with it. | The island's rangefinder ignores trees, crew and vehicles. | The divergence table in [spotting and ranging](field-guide/practices/spotting-and-ranging.md#where-the-island-differs). |
| Binoculars see through haze or fog. | Binoculars magnify the light that reaches them; air shimmer, and probably haze (still unsourced), blur a distant view. | Island fog is pushed back while the binoculars are up. | The divergence table in [spotting and ranging](field-guide/practices/spotting-and-ranging.md#where-the-island-differs). |
| A compass bearing can go straight onto a map. | A compass points to magnetic north. Declination separates it from true north, and grid north on a map can differ again, so lookouts correct a compass bearing before plotting it. | The island has one north, with no declination. | [Spotting and ranging](field-guide/practices/spotting-and-ranging.md#compass-bearings). |
| Bracken is harmless. | It is poisonous to livestock, and its stands harbour ticks. | Island bracken is soft scenery with no effects. | The [bracken explainer](field-guide/places/bracken-slopes.md). A tick-check line could come from a resident. |
| Land recovers from fire in days. | Recovery takes seasons to decades. | Island burn scars recover over three dawns. | The wildfire divergence table names the time compression. |
| Glacial valleys and fjords can be small. | Real glacial troughs and fjards are usually kilometres long (still unsourced). | The island's glacial valleys are 13–16 m deep on an island of about 0.2 km² with a summit near 50 m. | The scale divergence note in [the island's deep history](design/island-story.md#2-ice-carves-the-valleys). |
| The prevailing wind in the Scottish Highlands blows from the south-east. | The Highlands' prevailing wind is south-westerly (still unsourced: needs a Met Office climate source), so flagged pines there lean north-east. | Starter Island's breeze blows from the south-east, so its ridge pines lean north-west. | The divergence note on the [exposed ridge](field-guide/highwood-habitats.md#exposed-ridge-toward-windstone-ridge) card. |
| A road only needs to be flat and firm. | Water is the main destroyer of forest roads. Real roads shed rain sideways with a crown or crossfall, catch it in ditches, and let it across in culverts and dips. | Island roads have no ditches or dips. Culverts are placed where water would pond, but not drawn, and roads never wash out. | The divergence table in [forest roads](field-guide/practices/forest-roads.md#where-the-island-differs). Ditches and drawn culverts are planned. |
| A shallow ford or flooded road is safe to cross. | Don't walk, cycle or drive into flood water. Even shallow moving water can knock you over, and it can hide debris and rocks. In an emergency, call 999. | The fire road crosses Alder Creek's bed, which never floods. | The safety box in [forest roads](field-guide/practices/forest-roads.md#fords), pending a human safety review. A ford that floods after heavy rain could teach staying out of the water. |
| A road's fill can hang off any slope. | Fill slopes stand at about 34°, and on side slopes over about 50–55% a road is cut wholly into the hill, with the spoil carried away. | Island roads bench into side slopes from about 45%, fully by 75%, but the dug spoil simply vanishes instead of being hauled away. | The divergence table in [forest roads](field-guide/practices/forest-roads.md#where-the-island-differs). Hauling spoil is not modelled. |

To add a row, start from a divergence note whose "what a player might wrongly learn" column is likely, then source *What is true*.
