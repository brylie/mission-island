# Highwood habitats

The Highwood is the planned north-west backcountry of Starter Island: a mosaic of native pinewood, heath, wet woodland and an exposed ridge, with a fire access road and the Fire Watch on a summit. Real places like this, such as Glen Affric or Abernethy in Scotland, change gradually from one habitat to the next. Soil, water, slope and wind decide what grows where. This page describes each habitat, how to recognise it and why it sits where it does. The plants are in [Highwood plants](highwood-plants.md), and terms in *italics* are in the [glossary](glossary.md).

The habitat map and placement belong to the terrain and placement work that follows [#196](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/pull/196). The [Starter Island biome plan](../design/starter-island-biomes.md) covers the island's other habitats.

## Pinewood interior

- **What you see.** Tall Scots pines with bare lower trunks and separate crowns, with occasional birch and rowan. Light reaches the ground in patches. Beneath, there's a carpet of bilberry, heather, moss and fallen needles.
- **Why here.** Poor, acid, free-draining soil and enough shelter for tall trees.
- **What changes it.** Pines only regenerate where light reaches the ground. In a healthy pinewood, gaps open when old trees fall, and young pines grow up in them. Old trees, *snags* and *deadfall* are a normal part of the wood, not a sign of neglect.
- **Life.** Red squirrels, crossbills, wood ants, crested tits and capercaillie in the real Caledonian pinewood.
- **In the game.** Sheltered mature and old pine; occasional birch and rowan; bilberry under the canopy. Bracken grows only in gaps. Saplings grow only in gaps and at edges.

## Edges and gaps

- **What you see.** Young pines and birches, bracken, juniper and a brighter, more varied ground layer where the canopy opens. This includes clearings, the fire road and the ground beside old windthrow.
- **Why here.** The extra light lets *pioneer species* in, and the ground is often disturbed.
- **What changes it.** *Succession*: birch comes first, and pine grows up through it over decades.
- **In the game.** Pine and birch saplings, bracken patches and deadfall logs belong here.

## Heath and juniper scrub (Juniper Hollow)

- **What you see.** Open ground of heather and grasses, with scattered juniper. The juniper grows in columns in shelter and spreading bushes elsewhere, with a few birch saplings at the edges.
- **Why here.** Thin, acid soils and grazing keep the ground open.
- **What changes it.** Without some grazing or disturbance, birch and pine slowly invade. With too much grazing, young juniper is eaten and the old bushes are never replaced.
- **In the game.** Juniper, about 45% of sheltered plants as vases, with heather in the open. The existing hares and foxes of Juniper Hollow use this cover.

## Exposed ridge (toward Windstone Ridge)

- **What you see.** Sparse, wind-bent pines that lean and stream away from the prevailing wind, with low, spreading juniper and short heather among bare rock.
- **Why here.** The wind dries and damages shoots on the windward side, so trees grow short and one-sided (*wind flagging*).
- **What changes it.** A storm can throw shallow-rooted trees, so the ridge collects more deadfall.
- **In the game.** Exposed pine, juniper and heather only, with no alder, aspen, willow or bracken. Exposed templates lean toward +x, so placement turns each instance to point downwind.

## Wet woodland (Mirror Pool and stream banks)

- **What you see.** Alder and grey willow along water and in soggy hollows, with mosses, sedges and rushes beneath.
- **Why here.** Waterlogged soil that pines can't tolerate. Alder fixes nitrogen and copes with flooding.
- **What changes it.** Water level. Wet woodland on permanently waterlogged ground is called *carr*.
- **In the game.** Alder and grey willow, with no pine. Silver birch belongs on drier ground. A downy birch for wet ground is [#212](https://github.com/brylie/mission-island/issues/212).

## Bracken slopes

- **What you see.** Dense stands of bracken on deeper, well-drained hillsides, green in summer, bronze in autumn and flattened brown in winter.
- **Why here.** Deeper soil and light. Bracken spreads underground by *rhizomes* and shades out smaller plants.
- **In the game.** Bracken patches on gentle slopes and in gaps, never on the ridge or in wet ground.

## Fire in the Highwood

The [Fire Watch](../mechanics/fire-watch.md) looks over this country.

- **Scots pine.** Thick lower bark protects mature trees from light ground fires.
- **Heather.** It burns readily, which is why moorland is sometimes burned deliberately in small, controlled patches.
- **Recovery.** After a fire, birch and bracken are often first back, and pine seedlings follow where there's a seed source.

The burn-scar stages in the Fire Watch study show the first part of that recovery.
