# Highwood habitats

**Type: reference (place cards). Status: in progress. Last tended 2026-11-23.** Highwood's land is on the island, and the game now divides it into these habitats from its soil water, height, slope, rock and wind exposure ([how](../backcountry.md#habitats)). Each habitat is planted with the species these cards name.

**New here?** Read the pinewood interior and the exposed ridge first: they're the Highwood's two extremes, and every other habitat sits between them. For processes that cross every habitat, see the explainers on [wildfire](processes/wildfire.md), [bracken slopes](places/bracken-slopes.md) and [wet woodland](places/wet-woodland.md).

The Highwood is the planned north-west backcountry of Starter Island: a mosaic of native pinewood, heath, wet woodland and an exposed ridge, with a fire access road and the Fire Watch on a summit. Its real-world models are the native pinewoods of the Scottish Highlands, such as Glen Affric and Abernethy. In places like those, soil, water, slope and wind decide what grows where, and one habitat grades into the next.

Each card uses the framework's [place shape](../knowledge-framework.md#entry-shapes):
- **What you see**
- **Why here**
- **What changes it**
- **Life**, which is the real world
- **In the game**, which is what a player will see
- **Notice this**, an observation to make

**Sources.** Each Life line names its sources by id in the [sources register](../sources.yaml). The sources were opened and quoted by the Knowledge Keeper on 2026-11-22, so the lines are **sourced**. They become *checked* once a person checks each quote against its page. Anything still marked *unsourced* or *partial* is listed at the end of the page.

Plants are described in [Highwood plants](highwood-plants.md), and terms in *italics* are in the [glossary](glossary.md).

## Pinewood interior

- **What you see.** Tall Scots pines with bare lower trunks and separate crowns, with occasional birch and rowan. Light reaches the ground in patches over a carpet of bilberry, heather, moss and fallen needles.
- **Why here.** Poor, acid, free-draining soil, and enough shelter for tall trees.
- **What changes it.** Gaps open when old trees fall, and young pines grow up in the light. Old trees, *snags* and *deadfall* are a normal part of a healthy pinewood.
- **Life (real).** In Caledonian pinewood: red squirrel, crossbill (including the Scottish crossbill, found nowhere else), crested tit nesting in holes in standing deadwood, the needle mounds of wood ants, and capercaillie in old forest with dense undergrowth. *Sources: wt-caledonian-forest, woodtrust-caledonian.*
- **In the game.** Tall pines, some old and broken-topped, with birch and rowan among them and bilberry beneath. Young trees appear only in the gaps.
- **Notice this.** Look up from a gap and find where the youngest pines are growing. Are any in deep shade?

## Edges and gaps

- **What you see.** Young pines and birches, bracken, juniper and a brighter, more varied ground layer where the canopy opens. This includes clearings, the fire road, and the ground around old windthrow.
- **Why here.** The extra light lets *pioneer species* in, and the ground is often disturbed.
- **What changes it.** *Succession*: birch comes first, and pine grows up through it over decades.
- **Life (real).** Edges and glades are among a wood's most valuable habitats: songbirds feed in the light, butterflies warm up in sunny clearings, and deer browse young trees, killing or deforming them. *Sources: fc-rides-edges (England), fls-deer-management.*
- **In the game.** Saplings, bracken patches and fallen logs gather in clearings and along the road.
- **Notice this.** Compare how many kinds of plant grow at the wood's edge with how many grow deep inside.

## Heath and juniper scrub (Juniper Hollow)

- **What you see.** Open ground of heather and grasses, with scattered juniper. Juniper grows as columns where it's sheltered and as spreading bushes elsewhere, with birch saplings at the edges.
- **Why here.** Thin, acid soils and grazing keep the ground open.
- **What changes it.** Without some grazing or disturbance, birch and pine slowly invade. With too much grazing, young juniper is eaten and old bushes are never replaced.
- **Life (real).** Common juniper has declined across the UK and often fails to regenerate. Its seedlings need open, disturbed ground, and browsing by deer and rabbits is partly to blame. Heath supports nectar-feeding insects, ground-nesting birds and common lizards. *Sources: woodtrust-juniper, plantlife-lowland-juniper (England), wt-heather, woodtrust-heathland, rspb-heathland.*
- **In the game.** Heather in the open and juniper in both upright and spreading forms. The hares and foxes of Juniper Hollow use the cover.
- **Notice this.** Find a sheltered juniper and an exposed one, and compare their shapes.

## Exposed ridge (toward Windstone Ridge)

- **What you see.** Sparse, wind-bent pines that lean and stream away from the prevailing wind, with low juniper and short heather among bare rock.
- **Why here.** Wind dries and damages shoots on the windward side, so trees grow short and one-sided. This is *wind flagging*.
- **What changes it.** Storms throw shallow-rooted trees, so the ridge collects more deadfall.
- **Life (real).** Exposed heath holds mosses, lichens and low, wind-pruned heath plants. Meadow pipits, common nesting birds of moorland and heath, breed on open hillsides. *Sources: jncc-h4060, which describes higher montane heath than the island's ridge; wt-meadow-pipit.*
- **In the game.** A few wind-bent pines all leaning the same way, with low juniper and heather, and no broadleaf trees or bracken.
- **Notice this.** Which way do all the ridge pines lean? That tells you where the prevailing wind comes from. On Starter Island the breeze comes from the south-east, so the pines lean north-west. In the Scottish Highlands the prevailing wind is south-westerly, so flagged pines there lean north-east (unsourced).

## Wet woodland (Mirror Pool and stream banks)

- **What you see.** Alder and grey willow along water and in soggy hollows, with mosses, sedges and rushes beneath.
- **Why here.** Waterlogged soil, which pines can't tolerate. See the [wet woodland explainer](places/wet-woodland.md).
- **What changes it.** The water level. Wet woodland on permanently waterlogged ground is called *carr*.
- **Life (real).** Alder enriches waterlogged soil, and otters, seed-eating finches and many invertebrates live in wet woods. Details and sources are on the [wet woodland](places/wet-woodland.md) page.
- **In the game.** Alder and grey willow by the water, with little or no pine, thinning toward the water.
- **Notice this.** Follow the stream and watch where the alders stop. Their edge shows how far the wet ground reaches.

## Bracken slopes

- **What you see.** Dense bracken on deeper, well-drained hillsides: green in summer, bronze in autumn, and flattened brown in winter.
- **Why here.** Deep soil and light. Bracken spreads underground by *rhizomes* and shades out smaller plants. See the [bracken slopes explainer](places/bracken-slopes.md).
- **What changes it.** Grazing, cutting and fire. Bracken spreads where management eases off.
- **Life (real).** Bracken is toxic to livestock and harbours ticks, and whinchats and pearl-bordered fritillaries use bracken slopes. Details and sources are on the [bracken slopes](places/bracken-slopes.md) page.
- **In the game.** Bracken patches on gentle slopes and in clearings. You can walk through them.
- **Notice this.** Look for where bracken gives way to heather uphill and to rushes downhill. It's a rough map of the soil.
