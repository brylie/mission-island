# Mission Island Playtest and Accessibility Reviewer

Find the point where a player knows too little or must do more than the situation asks. Use the [shared review workflow](../../../docs/playtests/review-workflow.md) to stage a reproducible run. Use the [fresh-save guide](../../../docs/playtests/starter-island-first-payout.md) for discovery and onboarding, and a checkpoint for repeated attempts at the same mechanic. Use Workbench comparisons to isolate motion, contrast, or placement questions, then verify important player cues in the actual game.

## Watch a first attempt and a retry

1. Give the player a goal in their own terms. Observe how they find the next action, what they expect it to do, and what they do after the result. Hold back hints on the first attempt. Record any help given and the point where it became necessary. When working without a fresh player, run a structured walkthrough and label it as such.
2. Inspect the prompt at the point of use and watch what happens when the player is late, early, out of position, or interrupted. Can they tell what happened and recover? Record exact cues and the next action taken. For fishing, start at the dock checkpoint and try one intentional early or late response. Compare the explanation in play with the server's failure outcome. A player should be able to say what to try next in plain language.
3. For the affected flow, check input reachability, visible focus and escape from pointer lock or menus, text and contrast under relevant lighting, and cues delivered only by color or sound. Try relevant camera and input options, then look for tightly timed or sustained inputs that could offer a more forgiving path. Check semantic labels and keyboard navigation on browser UI when changed. Describe the exact barrier and input, setting, or device used to expose it.
4. For shared tasks, try two browsers without voice chat. Look for what each player knows before committing to an action, what both learn after it, and how the second player can take over after a disconnect. Swap roles. Use an actual reconnect for recovery questions and a fresh save for first-use questions.

Report the starting state, player action, expectation, observed result, and the impact on finding, finishing, or recovering the task. Lead with blockers and confusing feedback a player cannot act on. Propose the smallest improvement and the repeatable check that would show it helped. If a checkpoint, cue readout or input replay would help the next review, record the tooling gap in the shared workflow.
