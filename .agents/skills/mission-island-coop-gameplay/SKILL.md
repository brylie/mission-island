# Mission Island Co-op Gameplay Designer

Build useful work for people who want to play together. Connect each job to a person, place, or need the crew can recognize. Read [crew progression](../../../docs/design/progression.md) for shared and personal state, and [learning through island work](../../../docs/design/learning-and-careers.md) for designing jobs that teach. Use the [shared review workflow](../../../docs/playtests/review-workflow.md) for the solo and two-seat comparison.

## Design the job and its next step

- Describe the job's purpose, the action that finishes it, and the visible result in the world. Lay out a complete solo route. Give a pair distinct work that changes the result or makes the job easier, such as a driver positioning the flatbed while a loader checks clearance and secures a crate. Check both viewpoints for the information needed to coordinate. Let players swap roles, and leave a way for one to finish if the other leaves.
- Map the effort and rewards from acceptance to payout. Watch for time spent waiting, repeated easy tasks that outpay more interesting ones, and parallel chores that make working together feel pointless. Keep the existing shared bank, career and Trust ownership. Show both players the contribution and pay once for a shared outcome. Keep tunable amounts and unlock conditions in the mechanic spec.
- Name one skill the player practises, one feedback cue that explains the result, and one different job where the player can use the idea again. Demonstrate through the resident, tool, site, or job itself. Offer practice with an easy recovery, followed by a checkout that asks the crew to use the skill under a meaningful new constraint. Give players a reason to try other roles after a shared qualification opens up.
- Vary the context around a familiar skill, such as approach, cargo, weather, or destination. Keep accepted work stable and completable and let crews choose work that fits the session. When proposing a career step, identify the added responsibility and the equipment or access it earns.

## Playtest cooperation and learning

Start from equivalent conditions in the shared harness. Complete the same job solo and in a pair, swap roles, and try one recovery case. Record who could act at each stage, where either player waited, whether the shared reward was understood, and what would make the crew choose the job again. For learning, look for a player applying the idea on a changed task with less prompting. The player's next action is evidence. A qualification badge establishes shared access.

A [Seabrook return load](../../../docs/design/second-shift.md) is a candidate review. Can a solo player place and load the truck without help? Can the partner do something useful and see how it helped? Can each player take the wheel, line up at a different receiving bay and explain what they checked? Record the job change separately from any checkpoint or readout that would let another reviewer repeat the trial.
